02 · Goal of the First Call

One goal. Not a single extra one.

Tell yourself before every call: I'm not calling to sell AI. I'm calling to book an audit. Anything that pulls away from that goal — prices, long tech explanations, arguments — works against you.

The winning-call formula

Hook with a question → surface the pain → offer to show the numbers for free → book a Zoom audit date. A date in the calendar means the call closed successfully. Everything else happens at the audit.

03 · Why We Sell the Audit

The audit is an easy "yes" that opens the door

Selling a package cold is hard: the client doesn't trust you, doesn't see their upside, gets scared by the price. The audit removes all of that at once.

No commitment

Free, no strings. Saying "yes" to an audit is far easier than saying "yes" to an invoice.

Positions us as experts

We come not to sell but to diagnose and show numbers. That's a consultant's stance, not a pushy vendor's.

Sets the ground

At the audit we already hold their real "before" numbers. The package sells itself because the pain is quantified.

What our "audit" means in plain words: a 30–40 minute Zoom where we look at how the client handles leads today (portals, response speed, CRM, database), find the leaks, and show what they cost in money — and what to do about it.

04 · Why It's Us

Behind us — people from real estate

Use this in the first seconds of the call — it dissolves distrust faster than anything. We're not "IT people who read about the market." The key people on the team spent 10+ years inside agencies and know the pain from the inside.

Today my focus is AI integration for real estate and development. I speak with the owner in the language of their funnel, not "neural networks."

When the client hears they're talking to someone who closed deals worth tens of millions, the tone of the conversation shifts. We name their pain first — and that instantly puts us above competitors who sell "bots."

05 · How to Open the Call

The first 30 seconds decide everything

Don't open with "we're a company that does AI." Open with them and the pain. Three openers that work:

Open through the pain

"Be honest with me — a lead that came in from Bayut on a Friday evening, when does it actually get its first reply?"

Open through expertise

"We're a team that spent 10+ years in real estate ourselves. We help Dubai agencies stop losing leads at night. A couple of minutes to see if it's relevant to you?"

Open through curiosity

"I can show you in 5 minutes how many leads your agency loses between the portals and the CRM. Owners are usually surprised by the number. Interested?"

06 · Script — Closing the Audit

The call framework up to "when works for you on Zoom"

Don't read it word for word. Keep it live, listen more. Every line's job is to move closer to booking the audit.

You · opener

"Hi! [Name], ScaleFlow. We're a team that spent 10+ years in real estate ourselves — we help Dubai agencies stop losing leads. Literally a couple of minutes?"

You · hook question

"Tell me — a lead from Property Finder on a Friday evening, when does it get its first reply?"

Client

"Well, whenever a manager sees it… sometimes Monday."

You · amplify the pain

"There it is. You already paid the portal for that lead, but you only capture the conversion from the ones who waited. And that's every night and weekend. Usually it's dozens of leads a month."

You · selling the audit (the key moment)

"Let's not guess. Let me run a free audit for you — on Zoom, about 30 minutes. We'll look at how your leads are handled today, where exactly they leak, and what it costs you in money. You'll walk away with concrete numbers even if you order nothing after."

Client

"Well… sure, I guess."

You · lock the date (don't let go!)

"Great. Does Wednesday at 11 work better, or Thursday afternoon? I'll send a Zoom invite and a short checklist of what to have ready — takes a minute."

Stop signal

If the client asks "so how much does it cost?" on the first call — don't quote package prices. Answer: "That's exactly what the audit is for — without your numbers any price is a shot in the dark. At the audit I'll show options for your situation." Then steer back to the date.

07 · Objections at the Audit Stage

What you'll hear when you close for the audit

I don't have time for an audit"

"I understand, that's why I'm offering 30 minutes on Zoom, not a meeting. In that time you'll get a number for how many leads and how much money leaks out — that pays back the half hour many times over. Morning or afternoon better for you?"

Move: turn time into upside, then straight back to picking a slot.
What's there to look at, we're fine"

"Great, if so the audit will confirm it and you'll carry on with peace of mind. But usually 'fine' means nobody counts the night leads. 30 minutes and you'll know for sure, not on a hunch."

Move: don't argue — flip the confidence into a reason to verify.
How much will it cost?"

"The audit itself is free. And the cost of a solution depends on what we find: sometimes one bot is enough, sometimes it's a CRM setup. That's why we look first. On Zoom I'll show options for your situation — quoting a price without that wouldn't be honest."

Move: audit is free, price is the audit's subject. Don't burn the pricing.
Just send me the info by email, I'll look"

"I can, but a deck without your numbers is just a brochure — you'll close it. The value is that at the audit I show your specific leaks. Let's do 30 minutes — if it doesn't land, you lose nothing. Wednesday or Thursday?"

Move: "send it by email" = a soft no. Steer back to a live slot.
We already have a bot / it's all automated"

"Great, so you get it. Then the audit will show whether your bot captures everything it could, or some leads still leak — we find that even with people who 'set it all up.' Half an hour to be sure."

Move: an existing bot → a reason to check its effectiveness.
08 · Lock the Audit

An audit is booked only when there's a date in the calendar

"The client seems interested" ≠ a booked audit. Close the stage cleanly:

  • A specific date and time — not "sometime this week," but "Wednesday 11:00." Offer a choice of two slots.
  • Zoom invite sent — right away, while on the call, so it lands in the calendar.
  • Prep checklist sent — what the client should have ready (CRM access, lead stats). This raises show-up rate.
  • Google Form sent — right after booking, send the client our intake Google Form (we provide the link). Their answers let us prep for the Zoom in advance so we walk in ready, not cold.
  • Logged in the CRM as "Audit booked" with a reminder a day before.
  • Value of the meeting stated — "you'll walk away with numbers even if you order nothing."

Why the Google Form matters

Once the client agrees to the audit, immediately send them our Google Form (link provided by us). It collects the key details about their business and lead flow so we can prepare for the Zoom as fast as possible — the more they fill in beforehand, the sharper and more convincing the audit. Frame it to the client as: "I'll send you a short form — 2 minutes — so we come to the Zoom already prepared for your situation and don't waste your time."

Mini-goal

A successful first call = an audit date in the calendar + an invite sent. Not "the client will think about it," not "I sent the info" — a booked Zoom.

09 · Phase 1 Rules

Do / don't on the first call

Do

  • Open with their pain, not with yourself
  • Sell only the audit
  • Offer a choice of two slots
  • Promise numbers for free
  • Lock the date in the calendar while on the call
  • Send the Google Form right after booking

Don't

  • Don't quote package prices
  • Don't show cases or pricing
  • Don't explain the tech at length
  • Don't argue with an objection head-on
  • Don't let it drift into "send it by email"
