Phase 2.
The audit is a presentation that closes.

The client already said "yes" to the meeting. Now we show their numbers, the cases and three packages — and close on a pilot with pay-for-result.

01 · Before the Call

5 minutes of prep = half the sale

Don't walk into Zoom with a blank slate. Gather context before the meeting so you talk about their agency, not in the abstract.

  • Open their portals and site — Bayut/PF profile, listing count, whether there's a chat/bot on the site.
  • Test whether their number replies — message their WhatsApp as a buyer and time the response. That's your ace at the audit.
  • Gauge the size — how many agents, what segment (rental / sales / off-plan).
  • Keep open: this page, the cases (PDF), the pricing (PDF). Packages are below on this page.
  • Set up the demo — if you have a live bot demo, have the link ready to show in action.

Power move

If you messaged their WhatsApp in advance and they didn't reply within an hour — show it on screen during the audit. "I wrote to you as a buyer yesterday at 20:15 — still no reply. That gap right there is costing you money." Nothing sells better than their own leak, live.

02 · Audit Structure

30–40 minutes on a clear script

Don't improvise the order. Lead the client along the route: pain in numbers first → then proof → then solution → then close.

Frame the meeting

"Today we'll look at how your leads flow, where they leak, I'll show similar cases and options. At the end — we decide what's next." You set the agenda, you lead.

~3 min

Capture the "before"

Use questions to surface the current picture: response speed, where leads fall, the state of CRM and database. Quantify the pain.

~10 min

Show the cases

2–3 relevant cases matched to their pain. Not all five — only the ones that hit their situation.

~7 min

Three packages

Present the solution as three tiers. Lead toward the package that fits their scale.

~8 min

Close on a pilot

Offer a pilot with pay-for-metric. Lock the next step and a date.

~7 min
03 · Capturing the "Before"

Quantify the pain in their own words

This is the core of the audit. The more concrete the "before" numbers, the easier the close. Ask and note on screen (the client watches the picture build):

Speed & channels

How many leads a month? From which portals? How fast does a manager reply on average? What happens at night and on weekends?

CRM & losses

Do all leads reach the CRM? Any duplicates across portals? Can you see which channel brings deals and which burns budget?

Database

How many contacts in the base? Is it worked at all? Or does it sit as dead weight?

Cost per lead

What do you pay per lead on Bayut/PF? → Multiply by the number of cooled leads = the loss in money.

You · translate into money

"Look: a lead costs you [X] dirhams. Each month roughly [Y] leads cool off at night and on weekends. That's [X×Y] dirhams you paid the portal for but never captured. Every single month. That's exactly what we close."

04 · Showing Cases

Proof — but only what's relevant

Don't run through all five cases. Pick 2–3 matched to the pain you surfaced. The showing formula: problem (recognizable) → what we did → result-as-mechanic.

01
Agency, Almaty — leads sank in the evenings
Three portals, leads hung until Monday, managers pulled clients to personal numbers. We deployed a WhatsApp bot + order in the CRM.
→ Reply in seconds 24/7 · 100% of leads in CRM with source
02
Developer sales team — 120 leads a day sorted by hand
Sorting ate 10–12 person-hours a day. We introduced scoring and routing.
→ Hot lead to a strong manager in minutes · triage done by the system
05
Developer — a 15,000 base lying as dead weight
Lead in the morning, call in the evening, client left for a competitor. Voice AI: instant dial-out + base reactivation.
→ Call right after the form · whole base worked by campaigns

How to pick the case for the client

Losing leads at night → case 01. Many leads drowning in a pile → case 02. Large dead base → case 05. Document flow, contract errors → case 04. Reception can't keep up (beyond real estate) → case 03.

Key line about honesty

"Notice — there's no 'grew 40%' in our cases. We guarantee the mechanic: reply in seconds, 100% of leads in CRM. Growth we measure on your pilot. Anyone promising an exact percentage blind is selling you marketing." This sets us apart from hype-vendors.

06 · Closing on a Pilot

Our ace — money on the metric

This removes the last fear — "what if it doesn't work." Before the start we take a baseline, fix target metrics in the contract — part of the fee is paid only when they're met.

You · pilot offer

"Let's not take it on faith. We run a pilot: first we fix your current numbers, set the targets in the contract. You pay part of the fee only when we hit them. Minimal risk for you — you pay for the result."

You · closing question

"Based on what we covered today, it makes most sense for you to start with [Package 1/2]. Let's lock it in: I'll prepare a proposal on your numbers and we start the pilot. How does that plan sound?"

Pilot metrics

Lead response time · lead → viewing · viewing → deal. We always guarantee the mechanic (reply in seconds, 100% of leads in CRM); growth we measure "before/after."

07 · Objections at the Audit

What you'll hear closer to price

It's expensive"

"Let's go back to your own number. We calculated it — you lose about [amount] dirhams a month on cooled leads. The package pays back in [timeframe]. And you can start with the basic one at $5,400 and expand once you see the result on the pilot."

Move: back to their own loss figure + offer the lower tier.

"Of course. So you have something to think on — I'll prepare a proposal on today's numbers, so you decide on facts, not memory. And since the main risk is 'what if it doesn't work,' we remove it with a pilot that pays for results. Shall I prepare it?"

Move: convert "think" into preparing the offer + de-risk with the pilot.
Do you guarantee the result?"

"The mechanic — yes, 100%: reply in seconds, all leads in CRM. An exact growth percentage nobody honestly guarantees blind — that's why we run a pilot with before/after measurement and pay-for-metric. You pay for the result, not the promise."

Move: honesty + pilot as the guarantee instead of an empty promise.
Let's start with the smallest one"

"Great call — that's how most work. We start with Package 1, close the main leak, you see the numbers on the pilot, then expand as results come in. Shall we set it up?"

Move: don't argue — this is our land-and-expand model. Close it.
08 · Lock the Deal

An audit is closed only with a concrete next step

  • A package chosen (or a direction) for their scale and numbers.
  • A pilot agreed — what we measure, which target metrics.
  • A date set for sending the proposal and the next touch.
  • Third-party costs stated — so there's no surprise on the invoice.
  • Logged in the CRM with a stage and deal value.

Audit mini-goal

A successful audit = the client agreed to a pilot and chose a package / direction, with a proposal date set. Not "they liked it" — a concrete next step with a date.
