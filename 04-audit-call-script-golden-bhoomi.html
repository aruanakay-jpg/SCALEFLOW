Revenue Audit Call · 35–45 minutes

The audit call,
line by line.

Read the bold lines close to word-for-word — they're built to make the prospect say "yes, that's us." Everything else is yours. Never present a package before they've agreed on the problem.

00:00Open3 min 03:00Audit15 min 18:00Roadmap10 min 28:00Investment7 min 35:00Close5 min

Before you dial

5 min prep

Twenty audits were booked and nobody showed. Most of that is fixed before the call, not on it.

RuleIf they haven't confirmed 2 hours before, call them. A no-show is almost always an unconfirmed booking, not a lost lead.

1 · Open

0:00 – 3:00
Say thisBefore sharing screen

Set the frame in three sentences

"Thanks for the time. Quick frame so you know what this is: I've reviewed how enquiries move through your agency, and I'll walk you through what I found — where enquiries stop moving and what it's costing you in deals."

"It takes about thirty minutes. At the end, if it makes sense, I'll show you what fixing it looks like and what it costs. If it doesn't make sense, you'll still leave with the audit — it's yours either way."

"One ask: interrupt me. If something I say doesn't match how the agency actually runs, tell me straight away — a wrong audit is useless to both of us."

Why it works. You've named the outcome, the length, the ending, and given them permission to disagree. Nobody has to wonder when the pitch starts.
Ask this

Two questions before you share the screen

Question 1"Before I start — has anything changed in the agency since we spoke? New people, new systems, anything?"
Question 2"And is it just you making this decision, or is there someone else who'd need to see this?"
Then share the screen and open the audit deck.
Don'tDon't ask "how's business?" or "tell me about yourself." You already did discovery. Repeating it tells them you didn't prepare.

2 · The audit

3:00 – 18:00 · audit deck, 12 slides

Your only job in this block: get them to say out loud that the problem is real. If they haven't agreed by slide 8, do not move on to the roadmap.

Slide 1Cover · 30 sec

"Your enquiries are not lost. They're forgotten."

"This is prepared for Golden Bhoomi specifically — your team size, your channels, your registration. It's not a template."

"The title is the finding. Nobody in your agency is throwing enquiries away. They're just not written down anywhere, so they get forgotten."

Slide 2Leak score · 2 min

The number

"Seventy-four out of a hundred. That's how much of a typical enquiry's journey through your agency happens outside any system you can see."

"To be clear about what this is and isn't: it doesn't calculate money you've lost — I'd be inventing that number. It measures process control. Higher means more is escaping."

"Anything above sixty is where enquiries go missing without anyone noticing. You're at seventy-four."

Then stop and ask"Does that number surprise you, or does it feel about right?"
Wait for the answer. Whatever they say, you win: "about right" means they own it, "surprising" means you have their attention. Don't fill the silence.
Slide 3Seven categories · 2 min

Where the score comes from

"Seven places an enquiry can stop. Every one is scored on how likely it is to lose momentum there."

"CRM control is ninety-five — because there isn't one. Follow-up is eighty-eight. Owner visibility eighty-six. Those three are the whole problem in one picture."

Then ask"Which of these seven would you have guessed was your worst?"
Their answer tells you which of the next four slides to spend time on. Speed through the others.
Slides 4–7The four leaks · 6 min

One leak per slide — read the headline, then ask the question

Slide 4 — "Enquiries live in ten separate phones. A client who calls an agent who's busy or driving isn't your agency's enquiry. It's that agent's enquiry."

Ask"If one of your agents left tomorrow, what would you still have from their clients?"

Slide 5 — "The deal is usually in the fifth contact. Most enquiries stop at the first, because follow-up happens for whoever the agent remembers — usually the loudest client, not the closest one to buying."

Ask"Who's due a call today? Could anyone on the team answer that without scrolling WhatsApp?"

Slide 6 — "With no assignment rule, an enquiry that touches two agents often gets worked by neither. The client just experiences silence, and calls the next agency."

Ask"Last week — did any client have to chase you for a reply?"

Slide 7 — "Right now the agency is managed on impressions. Coaching happens after a bad month instead of during a slow week."

Ask"How many live enquiries does the agency have right now, this second?"
This is the moment. They can't answer that last one. Let the pause sit. Then: "That's the whole audit in one question."
Don'tDon't rush all four. Two done properly, with the prospect answering out loud, beats four narrated at speed.
Slide 8The volume question · 2 min

Handle "but I need more leads" before they raise it

"Now — you told me enquiries are irregular, mostly contacts and referrals. That's real, and it's the second problem, not the first."

"If we bought traffic into the setup we just looked at, you'd be paying to lose enquiries faster. So the order is: convert what already arrives, reactivate the contacts you already have, then test paid channels — with a system that can actually prove what each one is worth."

Ask"If we doubled your enquiries next month, could the team handle them the way things work today?"
Almost everyone says no. That "no" is what makes the next twenty minutes possible.
Slides 9–11After · priorities · quick wins · 3 min

Move faster here — they're already convinced

Slide 9 — "Same ten people, one system apart. Left is today, right is after. Nothing on the right requires hiring anyone."

Slide 10 — "The order matters more than the speed. Each step is worth less if the one before it hasn't happened."

Slide 11 — "Three things you can do this week without me, free. Write down every open client today. Agree a reply standard with the team. Hold new ad spend for four weeks."

Give the free advice properly. It's the strongest trust move on the call, and the first one is the one that hurts — a shared sheet of open clients shows them the gap themselves.
Slide 12The agreement gate · 1 min

Get the yes before you go anywhere near the plan

Ask this exactly"So — does this match how the agency actually runs?"
Yes → "Good. Then let me show you what fixing it looks like." Open the roadmap deck.
Partly → "Which part is off?" Fix it out loud, then ask again. Do not proceed until you get a clean yes.
No → Stop. "Then I've got it wrong — walk me through how it actually works." Reschedule if needed. A corrected audit sells; a disputed one never does.

3 · The roadmap

18:00 – 28:00 · roadmap deck, 9 slides

Switch tabs. The shift in tone should be obvious — you've stopped diagnosing and started building.

Slides 1–2The route · 2 min

"Four weeks. Three phases, and they run in this order because each one depends on the one before it."

"Watch the road at the bottom — it builds as we go. That's genuinely how the work runs: you don't get everything on day thirty, you get something working at the end of every phase."

Slides 3–5The three phases · 5 min

For each phase, read the outcome box, not the bullet list

Phase 1 — "By the end of week two: you open one screen and every live enquiry in the agency is there, with a name against it and a next step with a date."

Phase 2 — "By the end of week three: an enquiry that comes in at eleven at night gets a reply in seconds, arrives qualified in the morning, and the viewing is already on someone's calendar."

Phase 3 — "By the end of week four: four numbers that tell you whether last week was good, before anyone tells you it was."

The bullets are there for them to read later. On the call, sell the three outcomes only.
Don'tDon't say "AI", "bot", "integration" or "workflow automation" more than you have to. Say what happens: replies, bookings, reminders, one screen.
Slides 6–8Ownership and honesty · 3 min

"Everything on this slide is yours — accounts in your name, data exportable. If we stopped working together, you keep the system."

"And here's what this doesn't do: it doesn't generate new enquiries, and I'm not going to promise you a number of deals. We record your real figures in week one and agree targets from those. Anyone quoting you a percentage before measuring is making it up."

This slide closes more deals than the previous five. Slow down on it. Nobody in this market expects to hear what a service doesn't do.

4 · Investment

28:00 – 35:00 · proposal page
Say thisThen share the proposal

Three packages, one recommendation

"Three ways to build it. I'll tell you which one I'd choose for you and why."

"Package one, from two thousand two hundred and fifty — CRM and WhatsApp capture. It stops the bleeding, nothing more."

"Package two, from four thousand eight hundred — this is the one I'd recommend, and it's exactly the roadmap we just walked through. Proper CRM build, capture, automation, and your dashboard."

"Package three, from eight thousand seven hundred — everything in two plus a real AI assistant that chats and books, and a first paid-channel test."

"On top of any of them, monthly support: a hundred and fifty, or five hundred on package three where the assistant runs continuously."

Say the price and stop talking. The next person who speaks should be them. Count to five in your head if you have to.
If they ask why

Justify with the rate card, never with a discount

"Every line on the breakdown is our published rate card with the launch discount already applied. Nothing was invented for this proposal."

"Licences, WhatsApp charges, AI usage and any ad budget you pay directly to the provider — no markup from us. I'll tell you the expected range before you sign up to anything."

NeverNever discount on the call to save a silence. If price is genuinely the blocker, move them down a package — the scope shrinks, the price doesn't get cut.

5 · Close

35:00 – 40:00
The two questions

Never ask "do you want to buy?"

First"Based on everything we've gone through today — does this feel like the right direction for the agency?"
Wait for a full answer. Don't stack the second question on top of it.
Then"Great. Shall we start implementation this week?"

If yes — "I'll send the agreement and invoice today. Kick-off is one hour with you and a lead agent, within three working days. Which day suits — Tuesday or Thursday?"

Book the kick-off on the call. A signed agreement with no date drifts; a date in the calendar pulls the signature.
If they need to think

Get a specific next contact, not "I'll get back to you"

"Of course. What specifically do you want to think about — the package, the timing, or the money? So I send the right thing over."

"I'll send everything in the next thirty minutes. Can we put fifteen minutes in the calendar for Thursday, just to answer whatever comes up when you've read it?"

RuleNever end a call without either a start date or a booked follow-up. "I'll get back to you" is a no with better manners.
After the callWithin 30 minutes

Send the email while the call is still warm

  • The audit — personalised, with their name on the cover
  • The roadmap — the three phases, week by week
  • The proposal — packages, scope, terms
  • One line from the call in the email body — a phrase they used, quoted back. It proves you listened
  • The agreement and payment link — same day if they said yes
Follow-up rhythm if no reply: day 2 a short WhatsApp — "any questions on the audit?"; day 5 one useful thing, no ask; day 9 a direct "should I close this off for now?" Then stop.

Objections

Answer, then return to the deck

I don't need a CRM, I need more leads.”

"Completely fair — and that's usually the second thing we do, not the first. Right now, if I sent you fifty enquiries tomorrow, where would they land?"

Then: "Every enquiry you buy costs money. Every one you already have is free. We fix the free ones first — it's the cheapest revenue in the agency."

My agents already follow up.”

"I believe you — the good ones always do. The question isn't whether they follow up, it's whether you'd know if they stopped."

Then: "That's what the dashboard is for. Not to police them — to show you which agent needs help before the month ends."

It's too expensive.”

"Compared to what — another agency's quote, or the revenue it needs to return?"

If it's budget, not value: "Then start with package one. It's the smallest thing that stops enquiries disappearing, and everything above it builds on the same foundation — nothing gets thrown away."

Never answer this with a discount.

I've tried something like this before and it didn't work.”

"What happened?" — then listen properly. Nine times out of ten the answer is that a tool was installed and nobody used it.

Then: "That's why training and written rules are in the package, and why the dashboard exists — you'll see within days whether the team is actually using it, instead of finding out in three months."

Can you guarantee more deals?”

"No, and I'd be careful with anyone who does. I don't know your close rate yet — nobody does, because it isn't measured."

Then: "What I can commit to is that in week one we record your real numbers, and from week four you'll see them every week. If the system doesn't move them, you'll know before I do."

Send me the proposal and I'll review it.”

"I'll send it within the hour either way. Before I do — which package should I put at the front, so you're not reading three?"

Then: "And let's put fifteen minutes in for Thursday. If it's a no after reading, tell me on that call and I'll close the file properly."
