Leak 01 · Capture

Enquiries live in ten separate phones.

A caller who reaches an agent who is busy, driving or off that day is not an enquiry your agency holds — it's an enquiry that agent holds.

Nothing behind that first contact is recoverable if the agent forgets, gets a better lead an hour later, or leaves.

Ask the roomIf an agent left tomorrow, what would you still have from their clients?
02

Leak 02 · Follow-up

The deal is in the fifth contact. Most stop at the first.

Property buyers rarely decide on the first conversation. Without a list that says who to contact today, follow-up happens for the clients an agent remembers.

Usually the loudest ones — not the ones closest to buying.

Ask the roomWho is due a call today? Can anyone answer that without opening WhatsApp?
03

Leak 03 · Ownership

Everyone assumes someone else has it.

With no assignment rule, an enquiry that touches two agents often gets worked by neither.

The client experiences this as silence — and calls the next agency on their list.

Ask the roomLast week, did any client have to chase you for a reply?
04

Leak 04 · Visibility

You're managing on impressions.

Without one screen showing reply time, open enquiries and overdue actions, coaching happens after a bad month instead of during a slow week.

Spending on ads before this is fixed multiplies the leak, not the revenue.

Ask the roomHow many live enquiries does the agency have right now?
05

The volume question

Enquiries are irregular. That's the second problem, not the first.

You told us there's no steady flow of enquiries — most come from existing contacts and referrals, with no channel producing them predictably.

That's real. But buying traffic into the current setup means paying to lose enquiries faster. The order we'd recommend:

  • Convert what already arrives — capture, own, follow up
  • Reactivate the contacts the agency already has
  • Then test paid channels, with a system that can prove what each one is worth
Ask the roomIf we doubled your enquiries next month, could the team handle them the way they are today?

Current vs. designed

The same ten people, one system apart.

How it runs today

  • An enquiry arrives on whichever phone is nearest
  • Qualification depends on who answered
  • The next step is remembered, not scheduled
  • Nobody is formally responsible for a client
  • Past enquiries are effectively gone
  • Performance is discussed, not measured

How it runs after

  • Every enquiry lands in one list, whatever the channel
  • The same four questions asked every time, automatically
  • The next contact sits on a task list with a time on it
  • One named owner per enquiry, assigned by rule
  • Every past client searchable and reachable again
  • Reply time, open work and status on one screen

Before we start

Three things worth doing this week — free.

Write down every open client

One shared sheet, today. Name, budget, area, last contact, next step. It will be uncomfortable reading — that discomfort is the audit.

Agree a reply standard

Pick a number the team commits to — say, ten minutes in working hours. You can't automate a standard that doesn't exist.

Hold new ad spend

Until enquiries are captured and owned, more traffic means more of the same loss. Hold the budget four weeks.
