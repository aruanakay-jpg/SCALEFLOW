Call Script
Better Parker · Raj Tomar

What to say on each slide. Left column is the slide, right is your words.

FOUR RULES FOR THE WHOLE CALL

  1. Every problem gets its fix in the same breath — but never a price. Most slides now carry a "WHAT FIXES IT" block — 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 11, 12 and 13. Say the problem, then say what solves it, then move on. No numbers. If he asks what it costs, you send him the written offer — see the "$" section. That document is where price lives.
  2. Ask a question, then shut up. The silence is only uncomfortable for you. He will fill it with what actually hurts.
  3. Package 02 is the recommendation. It gets named once on slide 9 — the ten thousand slide — and again on slide 14. Don't sell all three. Show him the one that answers his goal, then let him compare.
  4. His words beat yours. When he describes his pain, write down his exact phrase — you'll hand it back to him on slide 14.
1
Cover
Better Parker Lead Audit
SAY
"Raj, thanks for the time. I'm not going to sell you anything for the next twenty minutes. I looked at how leads move through your business right now, and I want to show you what I found. If it's useful, we'll talk about what's next. If not, you got a free audit. Fair?"
WHY

He's expecting a pitch and he's already defensive. You take that away in one sentence. Then actually don't sell — the diagnosis does the work.

2
The reframe
You're about to BUY 3,000 leads — not win them
SAY
"You told me you're expecting about a hundred leads a day. Three thousand a month. Let's be precise about what that means — you're not going to win three thousand clients. You're going to buy three thousand leads.

Every one of them costs you money the second it arrives, whether anyone works it or not. A lead isn't a client. It's a receipt.

So the only question that matters is — how many of those three thousand do fifteen agents actually turn into deals?"
THEN

Stop talking. Wait. This is the most important question of the call. Let him start explaining — and let him hear for himself that there isn't an answer yet.

WHY THIS REFRAME IS THE WHOLE CALL

"Buy, not win" changes what every later slide means. Once a lead is a receipt, every slow reply is money he already spent and threw away — not a missed opportunity, a refund he'll never get. That reframe is what makes slides 3 through 13 hurt. Get it landed or the rest is just features.

Do not answer for him. Even if the pause runs ten seconds. He has to hit this wall himself.
3
Six ways a paid lead dies
The leak between the lead and the deal
SAY — WALK THE LIST, DON'T RUSH IT
"So here's every way a lead you already paid for dies. Six of them.

One — nobody answers for two hours, and he's already with the agency that replied in five minutes.

Two — your agents take the easy ones. They're on commission, they're rational people, of course they do. The rest never get worked, and nobody ever says so out loud.

Three — he replies once and goes quiet, and nobody follows up. Almost nobody ever does.

Four — he's not ready this month. In six months he is. But by then he's forgotten your name, because nothing kept in touch.

Five — the lead never reaches your CRM at all. It's on an agent's personal phone. He leaves, it leaves with him.

And six — you can't see any of this happening. Three thousand quietly becomes three hundred, and no report tells you where the rest went."
THEN — THE LINE THAT MATTERS
"Every one of those is already paid for. You don't have a lead problem. You've got a leak between the lead and the deal."
WHY THIS SLIDE CARRIES THE CALL

Six leaks, and each one maps to something you sell — instant reply, routing, follow-up, nurture, CRM capture, the dashboard. He doesn't know that yet. He just knows he's bleeding. Every later slide is picking one leak and closing it.

You're not attacking his agents — you're defending them: "they're rational people." So he won't argue. The problem sits in the system, not the people.

ASK
"Which of those six is happening to you right now?"
Then stop and let him pick one. Whichever he names is the leak he already knows about — that's your whole call. Write it down and hang every later slide on it.
4
Two to three hours
"You told us it could be instant"
SAY
"You told me a new lead waits two to three hours for a first reply. And you also told me it could be instant. That gap right there is what this whole call is about."
WHY

These are his words, not yours. He can't argue with himself. You're just handing it back.

Pause three seconds before you touch the fix. Let the gap sit. If you rush to the solution you're rescuing him from a feeling he hasn't finished having.
THEN — THE FIX ON THE SAME SLIDE
"So here's what changes. An AI answers every enquiry in seconds — any hour. It asks the budget, the area, the timing, and drops the lead into your CRM already qualified. Your agent stops starting cold."
No price here. If he asks — "and what does that cost?" — say: "It's in the written offer, I'll send it today. Let me show you the rest first." Then carry on. He'll ask again at the end, and that's the right time.
5
The race
Same buyer messaged four agencies
SAY
"Here's why those two hours are expensive. That buyer didn't only message you — he messaged four agencies at the same time. Whoever answers first owns the conversation.

Three hours later your agent calls someone who has already booked a viewing with somebody else. Same lead. Same cost to you. You just paid to arrive second."
ASK
"How often do your agents hear — thanks, I'm already working with someone?"
THEN

Be quiet and listen. This is where he names his pain in his own words. Write that phrase down — you're giving it back on slide 14 when you show the stages.

6
Nights
Buyers message after 8pm
SAY
"And here's the part that makes it worse. Most property enquiries in Dubai come in after eight in the evening — when the office is closed. So two hours of waiting becomes the whole night.

You have a team in India, which helps with time zones. But nights and weekends are still open on both sides."
IF HE ASKS FOR THE SOURCE

Dubai real estate industry data, TechBullion 2026 — roughly two thirds of enquiries land between 8pm and 2am. There's a footnote on the slide.

This is the key emotional slide. He should feel he's losing money every single night — not vaguely "sometimes."
THEN — THE FIX
"The AI doesn't sleep. Eleven at night on a Saturday is the same as Tuesday lunchtime. No night shift, no gap in the India rota, no Monday morning backlog to dig out of."
7
You call them
Outbound is his real channel today
SAY
"Now — one thing you said that I want to come back to. Today you don't really wait for leads. Your team calls them. That's your main channel, and it's the thing you're actually good at.

So when a lead does land, who calls it first? And how fast? Right now that depends on which agent is free — and whether it's a Tuesday or a Saturday night."
ASK
"When a new lead comes in — how long before someone actually picks up the phone and calls him?"
WHY THIS SLIDE EXISTS

This is the slide that earns the second stage. Without it, an AI that makes calls sounds like an upsell nobody asked for. With it, it's the obvious extension of what he already does — you're not changing his sales model, you're removing the delay from it.

Don't mention AI calling yet. Just plant the gap: the calling works, the speed doesn't. He'll connect it himself on slide 14.
8
What we found
CRM green · website · Instagram · portals
START WITH THE GOOD NEWS
"First, the good part. You already run a CRM and your team works in it. That's a real head start — most Dubai agencies don't have that."
THEN VERIFY IT — DON'T ASSUME
"You told me every lead and every update gets tracked in the CRM. Can I push on that for a second? Commission pays your agents for closing, not for logging. In most agencies that means some leads live on the agent's personal phone until they're worth logging. Is that happening here?"
WHY THIS MATTERS

Our IT lead flagged this and he's right: "they're on commission so they log everything" doesn't hold up. Commission usually pushes the other way — an agent hides a lead so it doesn't get reassigned. If leads are sitting on phones, the CRM data is incomplete and it changes what we build first. Find out now, not after the contract.

Don't argue with him if he insists it's all logged. Just say: "Good — then the first two weeks will confirm it, and we build straight on top." The measurement will tell the truth either way.
THEN
"Now what we found. Your website only has a contact form — no WhatsApp, no chat. Your Instagram says 'DM for expert advice,' and those DMs wait for a human to open them.

And we couldn't find you on Bayut or Property Finder. Is that right?"
The portals are a QUESTION, not a statement. A profile can exist without showing in public search. State it as fact and be wrong, and you lose all credibility in one second.
9
About the 10,000
"You can't hire your way there"
SAY
"You said you want to get to ten thousand agents. Let me be direct: you can't hire your way there.

Right now one manager hands out leads by hand. At fifteen agents that works fine. At a hundred and fifty it doesn't. Leads start sitting, and your good agents leave — because they're not getting enough."
ASK
"At what number of agents do you think handing out leads starts breaking for you?"
THEN — THE FIX, AND THIS IS THE IMPORTANT ONE
"So here's what fixes it. The leads score themselves and route themselves — by area, and by what each agent actually closes. The hot ones land with your best closers automatically. Nobody hands anything out.

That's the piece that carries you past fifteen agents. And honestly, that's the one I'd point you at."
WHY THIS IS THE MOMENT

This is his dream, and you're the only person who's explained why it won't work the way he thinks. Name the middle option here — once, plainly, then stop. By the time he sees all three on slide 14 he already knows which one he wants, so the table becomes a comparison instead of a decision from scratch.

Still no number. "That's the one I'd point you at" is the whole pitch. If he asks what it costs — "Full costings are in the offer, I'll send it today."

The point
"Build the machine. Then hire into it."
SAY
"So the real question isn't how many people you hire. It's what each new agent plugs into.

Build that first, and every agent you add actually works. Add agents without it and they leave. Build the machine, then hire into it."
WHY

This is the bridge from pain to solution. You've still sold nothing — you've only reframed his goal so the solution becomes obvious.

11
The database they already paid for
Money sitting in the CRM right now
SAY
"Before we chase a single new lead — there's money sitting in your CRM right now. While your team has been working outbound, leads piled up. Some went cold. Some replied once and nobody followed up.

You already paid for every one of them. That's not new business. That's business you bought and never collected.

Follow-up goes back through that database automatically — new launches, price changes, a tenancy expiring. The ones who are ready raise their hand, and your agent picks up a warm conversation. No agent hours."
ASK
"Roughly how many leads are in your CRM that nobody has messaged in the last month?"
THEN — THE FIX
"And the follow-up runs itself. New launches, price changes, a tenancy about to expire — sent by segment, not blasted. That's included in every option, by the way. It's the one thing that pays off on leads you already bought, in week one."
WHY THIS IS YOUR STRONGEST CARD

Everything else you're selling pays off later — on the next lead, next month. This one pays off in week one, on leads he already bought. It's the easiest yes in the whole call, and it's the cheapest thing to prove.

If he says "we don't really have a database" — that's fine, don't push. It means Package 01 is even more urgent, because right now nothing is being kept.
ONE THING TO BE STRAIGHT ABOUT

The sequences are built into every package. The ongoing work — segmenting, testing, tuning what gets sent — is a monthly retainer, and it's optional. Don't hide it: "The build is included. If you want us running and testing the campaigns month to month, that's a retainer on top — it's in the document." He'll respect that more than finding it later.

12
Leads walk out with agents
Commission pays for closing, not for logging
SAY
"Here's leak number five, and it's the one owners hate most. Your agents are paid to close, not to log. So the good leads live on a personal phone until they're worth logging — and some never are.

Then he leaves. And your leads leave with him."
ASK
"Has that happened to you — an agent leaves and the pipeline goes with him?"
THEN — THE FIX
"The way you fix that isn't a policy. It's making sure the lead lands in your system before your agent ever sees it. The AI captures it, qualifies it, logs it — automatically. Nothing to forget, nothing to keep. Your database stays yours whoever walks out."
WHY THIS LANDS HARD

Every Dubai brokerage owner has lived this, and with commission-only teams it's constant. It's also the cleanest argument for capture-before-agent: you're not policing his agents, you're removing the temptation. Say it once and let it sit.

Careful here. Don't imply his agents are dishonest — he'll defend them and you'll lose the room. It's a system flaw, not a character flaw. "They're paid to close, not to log" is the frame.
13
The dashboard
Which platform actually pays? · leak six
SAY
"And leak six — right now you can't see any of this happening.

You're about to spend real money on lead sources. Which one brings deals, and which one brings noise you pay for anyway? Everything's entered by hand today, so the answer depends on whoever remembered to type it in.

You can't see how fast anyone answered — the number your whole business turns on isn't measured. And you can't see which agent actually converts. Only who talks loudest on Monday."
THEN — THE FIX
"One screen. Every lead from the first message to the signed deal, tracked without anyone typing anything. Cost per lead by channel, response time, conversion by agent and stage. You stop guessing which portal to renew and start knowing."
WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN IT SOUNDS

He's scaling to 10,000 agents. Every decision he makes from here is a spending decision — which portal, which channel, which agent to keep. Right now he'd be making all of them blind. This is the slide that makes the dashboard feel like infrastructure instead of a nice chart.

ASK
"Today, if I asked you which platform brings your best deals — could you answer that from a screen, or would you have to ask around?"

HOW TO PICK WHICH ONE TO PUSH

You already named 02 on slide 9 — this slide is the comparison, not the pitch. He's had four slides to sit with the routing idea, so let the table do its job: three columns, one already marked as yours. 01 is the safe fallback if he flinches. Only push 03 if he tells you the three thousand is already flowing and he's live on the portals. Selling him volume he doesn't have yet is how you lose him at invoice two.

This is where he'll ask the price — and that's fine, it's the right moment. Answer with the "$" section below: no number, send the offer today. The deck has done its job; the document does the rest.
Don't mention the upgrades here. Voice calling, the knowledge base, permit checks, document autofill — they're all in the document, below the packages. If you list them on the call you're selling four things at once and he decides none of them. The slide already says they come later.
15
How we de-risk it
Measure first · write it into the contract
SAY
"Here's how we make this safe for you. Before we build anything, we measure your numbers as they are today — how long a lead really waits, how many reach a viewing, how many close. We put those numbers in the contract. Then we measure against them.

If nothing changes, we keep working at our cost until it does. We don't promise 'plus forty percent leads' — numbers like that are just marketing."
WHY

Kills the main objection — "what if it doesn't work" — before he's had a chance to say it out loud.

If he goes quiet after this slide — you go quiet too. The next person to mention money should be him.
16
Three questions
Portals · lead source · CRM
SAY
"To give you an exact quote instead of a range, I need three answers. Let's do it now:

One — are you live on Bayut and Property Finder?
Two — those three thousand leads a month, where are they coming from?
Three — which CRM are you on, what's it called?"
AND TWO MORE, IF YOU HAVEN'T ASKED THEM YET

Last one — your team in India, do they work the same leads as the Dubai team?"
WHY THE INDIA QUESTION

If client data moves between the UAE and India, UAE data law needs a lawful basis for it. We handle it before launch, not after — but we need to know now, because it affects the build. Don't explain the law to him on the call; just get the answer.

CLOSE
"Perfect. I'll send you the full offer with numbers today — you can show it to your partners. Everything we discussed, plus the exact quote."
WHY

The call ends with his answers, not your pitch. He hangs up feeling he's already started — not that he's been worked.

$
If he asks "how much?"
Any slide, any time — same answer: send the offer
SAY
"There are three, and which one you need depends on your CRM and your portal setup — that's exactly what I'm asking you about. I'm not going to throw a number at you on a call. I'd rather be accurate than fast.

Answer my three questions and I'll send you the full thing today with exact figures. You'll have it in writing before you have to think about it."
WHY NOT JUST SAY IT

A number said out loud has no context around it. He hears it, reacts to it, and starts negotiating before he's seen what's inside. In the document the price sits underneath the value, where it belongs — and he can show it to his partners without you in the room.

He may ask early — on slide 4 or 6. That's a buying signal, not an obstacle. Same answer every time: "It's in the written offer, I'll send it today. Let me show you the rest first." Then keep going. Asking twice means he wants it.

THE PRICING SLIDES · SLIDES 17–18

There are two price slides at the very end, after the natural close. You don't present them by default — the deck ends on slide 16 with your three questions. Bring them up only when he asks to see numbers, or when he's answered your questions and is ready to compare. Slide 17 is the three cards; slide 18 is the tick-by-tick of what's in each.

When you do open them, land on 02. It's marked "our recommendation" and it's the middle column — the eye goes there anyway. Say: "This is the one I'd start you on." Then stop talking and let him read. The written offer with the full breakdown still goes out today regardless.
If he pushes — "just give me a ballpark" — say: "Starts in the mid four figures and goes to low five depending on which of the three you need. My questions decide that. Exact breakdown today." Then move on. Don't elaborate, don't defend it.
IF HE ASKS FOR A DISCOUNT
"The small version is already the smallest thing that actually works — I've scoped it down rather than priced it down. If I cut it further it stops answering your leads properly, and then neither of us has anything to measure."
Never cut the price. Cut the scope. Dropping the number because he asked tells him it was never real — and he'll ask again on every invoice. Package 01 is the discount — it's the same starting point for less than half of 03. That's your answer, and it's true.

Objections
Have these ready
We already have a CRM. Why do we need this?
"A CRM is a warehouse. It stores the lead once it's yours. We're talking about the first two minutes — before it becomes yours at all. Your CRM is good. We're not replacing it, we're feeding it."
Our agents manage fine.
"Today, yes — at today's volume. The question isn't about today. It's about the day a hundred leads arrive instead of ten. Fifteen people physically can't answer a hundred buyers in five minutes. Not because they're bad. Because there aren't enough of them."
A bot will scare clients off. People want a human.
"Agreed — at the deal stage they want a human. But at eleven at night the choice isn't between a bot and a human. It's between a bot and silence until morning. The bot doesn't close the deal — it holds the conversation while your agent sleeps."
A robot calling my clients? That's not how we do business.
"Fair — and that's why it's not in either package. It's an upgrade you'd add later, after you've seen the AI answering your leads and you trust it. And it says it's an assistant in the opening line — that's the rule here, and we follow it. It's not pretending to be your agent. It's making sure someone picks up in ten seconds instead of three hours, then handing your agent a warm conversation."
Too expensive / no budget right now.
"Understood. Let's look at it differently: what do you pay for one lead? And how many a month don't get answered in time? We're not adding a cost. We're recovering what you already paid for."
I need to think / discuss with my partners.
"Of course. I'll send the offer as a document so you can show them — everything with numbers. One question: what needs to be in that document to make the decision easy?" → then listen. He'll tell you the real objection.
How much of my team's time will this take?
"Almost none. We need access to your CRM and one hour of your time to agree the questions the AI asks. After that we build it. Your team sees the result, not the work."
Our old leads are dead, there's no point messaging them.
"Some are. But 'dead' usually means 'nobody followed up,' not 'not interested.' A buyer who enquired six months ago and didn't buy is often still looking — they just bought from whoever stayed in touch. We're not cold-calling them. We're giving them a reason to reply: a new launch, a price change. It costs you nothing to find out — the sequences run themselves."
What if our agents don't use it?
"They don't have to change anything. The AI works before the agent — it answers, qualifies, and drops a ready lead into the CRM they already use. If anything, they get fewer bad calls. Commission-based agents adopt this fast, because it puts better leads in front of them."
Why not build everything at once?
"You can — but I wouldn't. The first build tells us what's actually true about your leads: where they come from, how many there really are, what your CRM holds. Everything else is scoped from those answers. Do it blind and you pay for guesses."
